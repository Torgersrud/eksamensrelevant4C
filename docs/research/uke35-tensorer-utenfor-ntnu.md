## 1. Question and level bar

**Question.** Which publicly available, non-NTNU exercise/exam material on tensor decompositions exists at the level of TMA4432 Matematikk 4C, "Uke 35 – Tensors and Tensor Decomposition", so a thin week can be filled with level-matched practice with known solutions?

**Level bar (uke 35).** Hand-computable problems on small tensors (2×2×2, 3×2×2): order/mode/entry/fiber/slice notation; outer products and rank-1 tensors; mode-n products and matricisation/unfolding; CP/CANDI decomposition (rank, fitting a small tensor as a sum of rank-1 terms); basic Tucker / multilinear-multiplication computations; storage-count arguments (full vs low-rank); simple applications (compression, profile data). Bachelor engineering-maths level, exam- or exercise-style, **with a known solution**.

**Reject / flag as too advanced:** research-level multilinear algebra, gradient-based tensor optimisation, HOSVD theory proofs, measure theory, pure application papers without exercises, physics tensor-networks material, abstract-algebra tensor products.

**Verification levels used below:**
- **FETCHED** – I downloaded and inspected the file/page myself this session.
- **INDEXED** – the source's own content was returned via a search-engine cache (content seen, server not hit directly).
- **LISTED** – known to exist from a catalog/link, content not seen.

## 3. Harvest list — what is realistically downloadable, level-matched, and answerable

Ranked; "with solutions" is the scarce resource — most external material omits published solutions.

1. **Ballard & Kolda, *Tensor Decompositions for Data Science*, final draft (users.wfu.edu).** One coherent exercise bank covering exactly the uke-35 arc (notation, matricisation, mode-n products, CP rank/fit, Tucker, storage counting, EEM/social-network applications). Pick the small-number hand-computable exercises from chs. 1–5; solutions can be generated and checked by hand or with Tensorly. *Solutions: must be self-produced (none public).*
2. **Bro's N-way course (ucphchemometrics.com/n-way-course/).** ~10 guided exercise sets on PARAFAC/Tucker/N-PLS with model answers embedded in the tutorial; free and explicitly licensed for use. Adapt the conceptual/interpretation tasks to 2×2×2 hand versions.
3. **Kolda & Bader survey, Sandia-report version (SAND2007-6702).** Not exercises, but the worked micro-examples (rank-1 sketches, mode-1 unfolding figure) are the cleanest legal raw material for writing new drill items; US-government text is reproducible with attribution.
4. **Eldén, *Matrix Methods*, ch. 8 (+ch. 14) — via library/instructor copy.** The only source whose *level* is a guaranteed bullseye for a Norwegian engineering-maths course (the book grew out of LiU's numerical-methods course, and LiU's current TNA010 still examines exactly this material). Exercises exist in the SIAM print edition per the series format — **verify in print; solutions manual is instructor-gated**.
5. **Tensorlab demos + user-guide examples (tensorlab.net).** Copy-ready "expected answer" computations (MLSVD, CPD, rank estimation) for verifying any hand-computed drill; amino-acid EEM dataset is the classic profile-data example matching uke-35's "profile data" theme.
6. **Huckle (TUM) slides tensor-kurs_1.pdf.** Model examples for notation/CP/Tucker at almost exactly the right technical depth; rewrite into problems.
7. **Tensorly documentation (https://tensorly.org/dev/user_guide/tensor_decomposition.html)** (BSD-3). Open-source worked CP/Tucker examples with printed outputs — answer-key material, not problems. (INDEXED; found in searches, not separately deep-verified this session.)
8. **LiU MAI exam-archive pattern (courses.mai.liu.se …/tentor.html)** — many maths courses publish *tentor med lösningar*; no tensor course is in the archive, but this is the place to **ask** MAI/IDA whether TNA010 past papers can be shared; same move works toward Chalmers/DTU lecturers.

## 5. Conclusion

**Realistic yield: modest — roughly 15–25 level-matched drill items**, not a public treasury. Specifically: about 10–15 hand-adaptable problems from the Ballard–Kolda exercise bank (solutions self-produced), 5–8 conceptual/interpretation tasks from Bro's free N-way course (answers included), and 5–10 problems synthesised from the Kolda–Bader Sandia-report examples, Huckle's slides and Tensorlab's worked outputs. The single highest-value *exam-style* item (LiU TNA010 past papers with solutions) exists as a course but is not on the open web; one email to the LiU examiner is the cheapest untested route.

**Best three bets:**
1. **Ballard & Kolda, *Tensor Decompositions for Data Science* (free draft)** — the only comprehensive, freely readable, scope-complete exercise set; re-word + self-solve.
2. **Bro's N-way course (ucphchemometrics.com)** — genuinely free, answer-bearing, application-flavoured, Nordic.
3. **Kolda & Bader Sandia report (SAND2007-6702) + Huckle TUM slides** — legally safe model examples to convert into uke-35-style drills.

**Could NOT verify (stated plainly):**
- That Eldén's ch. 8 actually ends with exercises, and any solution availability (SIAM print check required).
- Any public TNA010 exam/solutions (course page not on MAI's archive server; direct URL probes returned 404).
- Any public tensor-decomposition exercise+solutions page from Aalto/Helsinki, KU Leuven ESAT courses, ETH/EPFL, or German "Tensorzerlegung" courses — searches found only lecture notes, software docs, PhD-level summer schools (DTU 02901, Chalmers FBOM116) and abstract-algebra tensor-product sheets.
- Whether the now Publishers monograph of Sidiropoulos et al. contains end-of-chapter problems (paywalled; the freely fetched arXiv TSP version does **not**).
- The intended referent of "CBU tensor toolbox" in the task brief (matched only to UiB's Computational Biology Unit, which has no tensor exam material).
- Contents behind login walls generally (Aalto Moodle, KTH, DTU year folders, LiU Inspera archives) — by definition unverifiable from outside.
