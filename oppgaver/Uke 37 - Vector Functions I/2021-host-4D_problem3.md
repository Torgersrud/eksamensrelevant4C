### **Problem 3**

Let the function $f \colon (0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ be given as

$$f(t) := \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}} \int_0^{\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t}}} e^{-z^2} \, \mathrm{d}z - \frac{1}{2}.$$

**a)** Prove that the equation $f(t) = 0$ has a unique solution $T > 0$. You can use the famous identity $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-z^2} \, \mathrm{d}z = \sqrt{\pi}$. Give an upper bound for $T$.

**b)** Do **one** iteration of Newtons method starting from the initial value $t_0 = \left(\frac{5}{4}\right)^2$.
