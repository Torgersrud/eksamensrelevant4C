### **Solution to Problem 1**

a) The product $A^{H}A$ is

$$A^{H}A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-1&2\\ 2&-1&0&1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\ 0&-1\\ -1&0\\ 2&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}6&4\\ 4&6\end{pmatrix}.$$

The eigenvalues of this matrix are the solutions to the equation $(6-\lambda)^{2}-(4)^{2}=0$, that is $\lambda_1=10$ and $\lambda_2=2$. Now,

$$\ker(A^{H}A-\lambda_1 I)=\ker\begin{pmatrix}-4&4\\ 4&-4\end{pmatrix}=\left[\begin{pmatrix}1\\ 1\end{pmatrix}\right],$$

that is, the line of $\mathbb{R}^2$ generated by the vector $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\end{pmatrix}^{T}$. Since we are interested in normalized vectors, we define

$$u_1:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\ 1\end{pmatrix}$$

Similarly, for $\lambda_2=2$, we have that

$$\ker(A^{H}A-\lambda_2 I)=\ker\begin{pmatrix}4&4\\ 4&4\end{pmatrix}=\left[\begin{pmatrix}1\\ -1\end{pmatrix}\right],$$

and we define

$$u_2:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\ -1\end{pmatrix}.$$

This defines one of the unitary matrices of the Singular Value Decomposition

$$P=(u_1|u_2)=\begin{pmatrix}1/\sqrt{2}&1/\sqrt{2}\\ 1/\sqrt{2}&-1\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}.$$

The singular values of $A$ are $\sigma_1=\sqrt{10}$ and $\sigma_2=\sqrt{2}$. And we define

$$v_1:=\frac{1}{\sigma_1}A(u_1)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{20}}\begin{pmatrix}3\\ -1\\ -1\\ 3\end{pmatrix},\quad v_2:=\frac{1}{\sigma_2}A(u_2)=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}-1\\ 1\\ -1\\ 1\end{pmatrix}.$$

This gives rise to the other unitary matrix $Q=(v_1|v_2)$. We have the three matrices that define the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of $A:$

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}3/\sqrt{20}&-1/2\\ -1/\sqrt{20}&1/2\\ -1/\sqrt{20}&-1/2\\ 3/\sqrt{20}&1/2\end{pmatrix},\quad\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{10}&0\\ 0&\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad P=\begin{pmatrix}1/\sqrt{2}&1/\sqrt{2}\\ 1/\sqrt{2}&-1\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}.$$

The SVD decomposition is

$$A=Q\circ\Sigma\circ P^{H}.$$

(b) It suffices to apply the formula $x=A^{\dagger}b$; where $A^{\dagger}$ is the Moore-Penrose inverse of $A:$

$$A^{\dagger}=P\circ\Sigma^{-1}\circ Q^{H}.$$

We first compute

$$Q^{H}\cdot b=\begin{pmatrix}3/\sqrt{20}&-1/\sqrt{20}&-1/\sqrt{20}&3/\sqrt{20}\\ -1/2&1/2&-1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}-1\\ 0\\ 0\\ 1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\ 1\end{pmatrix}.$$

We conclude

$$x=A^{\dagger}b=\begin{pmatrix}1/\sqrt{2}&1/\sqrt{2}\\ 1/\sqrt{2}&-1\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1/\sqrt{10}&0\\ 0&1/\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0\\ 1\end{pmatrix}$$

$$=\begin{pmatrix}1/\sqrt{2}&1/\sqrt{2}\\ 1/\sqrt{2}&-1\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0\\ 1/\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\ -1/2\end{pmatrix}.$$

*Kjeldevarsel: i fasiten er brøken $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ trykt utan brøkstrek som «$-1\sqrt{2}$» i alle førekomstar av $P$ (og i utrekninga av $x$); leddet skal vere $-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$. Elles er alle matrisene kontrollreikna ($A^{H}A=\begin{pmatrix}6&4\\4&6\end{pmatrix}$, eigenverdiar $10$/$2$, $Q\Sigma P^{H}=A$, $A^{\dagger}=P\Sigma^{-1}Q^{H}$, $x=A^{\dagger}b=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&-1/2\end{pmatrix}^{T}$).*
