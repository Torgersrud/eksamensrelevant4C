### **Problem 7** **Fourier Transform [10 pts]**

Suppose the function $f$ is integrable and even. Consider the function $g(t)=\cos(at)$. Show that for all $a\in\mathbb{R}$, we have that the convolution product

$$(f*g)(t)=\cos(at)\hat{f}(a).$$

Recall that $\hat{f}(a)=\mathcal{F}(f)(a)$ is the Fourier transform of $f$ evaluated at $a$.

Hint:  You may use the trigonometric identity $\cos(x-y)=\cos(x)\cos(y)+\sin(x)\sin(y)$.

**For Mathematics 4D**: there should be an extra factor $\sqrt{2\pi}$ in the answer (i.e. they should show $(f*g)(t)=\sqrt{2\pi}\cos(at)\hat{f}(a)$) because the convolution was defined without $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}$ in the lectures.
