### **Solution to Problem 7**

We can use the convolution theorem

$$(2 \text{ P}.)$$

$$\hat{h}(\omega) = \sqrt{2\pi} \hat{g}(\omega) \cdot \hat{g}(\omega) = \sqrt{2\pi} (\hat{f}(\omega))^2$$

$g(x) = e^{-2x^2}$, i.e. the given function $f$ with $a = 2$.

$$(1 \text{ P}.)$$

From the formula sheet we know $\hat{g}(\omega) = \frac{1}{2} e^{-\frac{\omega^2}{8}}$

$$(1 \text{ P}.)$$

So we obtain for $\hat{h}$

$$(2 \text{ P}.)$$

$$\hat{h}(\omega) = \sqrt{2\pi} \left(\frac{1}{2} e^{-\frac{\omega}{8}}\right)^2 = \sqrt{2\pi} \frac{1}{4} e^{-\frac{\omega^2}{4}} = \frac{2}{4}\sqrt{\pi} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} e^{-\frac{\omega^2}{4}}$$

with is up to the first two factors the function $f$ with $a = 1$

$$(1 \text{ P}.)$$

$$\hat{h}(\omega) = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2} \mathcal{F}\left(e^{-x^2}\right)$$

and hence $h(x) = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2} e^{-x^2}$.

$$(1 \text{ P}.)$$
