### **Problem 9** (Kronecker and Khatri–Rao products at work: mixed products, Grams and selections, 8 points)

Throughout, use the conventions of Problems 2 and 8: $A\otimes B$ is the block matrix $(a_{ij}B)$, and the Khatri–Rao product $A\odot C$ of two matrices with the same number of columns stacks the Kronecker products of corresponding columns, $A\odot C=\big(\mathbf{a}_1\otimes\mathbf{c}_1\ \ \mathbf{a}_2\otimes\mathbf{c}_2\ \cdots\big)$. Consider the four matrices

$$A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\ 0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\ 2&1\\ 0&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad C=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\ 1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad D=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\ 1&2\end{pmatrix}.$$

a) Compute $A\otimes B\in\mathbb{R}^{6\times 4}$ and $C\otimes D\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}$ and multiply them. Separately compute $AC$ and $BD$ and form $(AC)\otimes(BD)$. Compare the two results, state the general law you have just verified (the mixed-product property), and say exactly which size conditions on $A,B,C,D$ make both sides defined and equal. (2 P.)

b) The product $A\odot B$ is defined because $A$ and $B$ both have $2$ columns. Compute $A\odot B\in\mathbb{R}^{6\times 2}$ and its Gram matrix $(A\odot B)^{\top}(A\odot B)$, and check it entry by entry against $(A^{\top}A)\circ(B^{\top}B)$. The two columns of $A$ are $\mathbf{a}_1=(1,0)^{\top}$, $\mathbf{a}_2=(2,1)^{\top}$ and those of $B$ are $\mathbf{b}_1=(1,2,0)^{\top}$, $\mathbf{b}_2=(0,1,3)^{\top}$; compute the off-diagonal Gram entry from the products $(\mathbf{a}_1\cdot\mathbf{a}_2)(\mathbf{b}_1\cdot\mathbf{b}_2)$ and state in one sentence what a nonzero off-diagonal entry says about the two rank-$1$ components in a CP fit (in Problem 5 c) both Gram matrices came out diagonal — what was special there?). (2 P.)

c) The Khatri–Rao product extends to any number of factors with a common column count. Compute the two iterated products $(C\odot B)\odot A$ and $C\odot(B\odot A)$ in $\mathbb{R}^{12\times 2}$ column by column and verify that they agree (associativity). Then verify that the first column of $C\odot B\odot A$ equals $\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{a}_1\circledast\mathbf{b}_1\circledast\mathbf{c}_1)$, the $12$-vector that stacks the entries of the rank-$1$ tensor with $i$ fastest, then $j$, then $k$, and do the same check for column $2$. Explain in one sentence why this is the mechanism behind the unfolding identity $\mathbf{X}^{(1)}=A\,(C\odot B)^{\top}$ of Problem 8. (2 P.)

d) Look back at $A\otimes B$ from a): the two columns of $A\odot B$ appear inside it — find which columns of $A\otimes B$ they are, and explain from the block structure $(a_{ij}B)$ why the answer is what it is. In general, for $M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ and $N\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}$, column $r$ of $M\odot N$ is column $(r-1)p+r$ of $M\otimes N$: state how many entries $M\otimes N$ has compared with $M\odot N$ and give the waste factor for $m=200$, $p=300$, $n=8$. Why does an ALS implementation never need to form either product? (2 P.)
