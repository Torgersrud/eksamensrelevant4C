### **Solution to Problem 11** (What the formats cost: CP and Tucker storage against the raw tensor, 8 points)

a) The cube has $320\cdot256\cdot120=9\,830\,400$ entries, i.e. $9\,830\,400\cdot8=78\,643\,200$ bytes $\approx78.6$ MB as `float64`. A rank-$6$ CP stores $6(320+256+120)=6\cdot696=4\,176$ numbers — a factor $9\,830\,400/4\,176\approx2\,354$. A Tucker format with core $6\times6\times6$ stores the core plus the three factors: $6^3+6(320+256+120)=216+4\,176=4\,392$ numbers — a factor $9\,830\,400/4\,392\approx2\,238$. The $216$ extra numbers are the full core: they let the $6$ components *interact* across modes (a general $6\times6\times6$ array of mixing weights) instead of forcing the superdiagonal structure of CP, which is what gives Tucker its mode-wise (multilinear) rank control. (2 P.)

b) A $d$-way Tucker format stores the core plus the $d$ factors:

$$\text{core}\ \prod_{k=1}^{d}r_k\quad+\quad\text{factors}\ \sum_{k=1}^{d}n_k r_k .$$

Here $\prod r_k=3^4=81$ and $\sum n_kr_k=3(100+80+60+40)=3\cdot280=840$, so $81+840=921$ numbers, a factor $19\,200\,000/921\approx20\,847$ against the raw data. A rank-$3$ CP stores $3\cdot280=840$ numbers (factor $\approx22\,857$): the CP format wins by exactly the $81$ core entries. (Note the CP winner can only be *cheaper*, never more accurate by construction — it is the more rigid format.) (2 P.)

c) A rank-$2$ CP is a core $(2,2,2)$ tensor whose only possibly nonzero core entries are the two superdiagonal ones $g_{111}$ and $g_{222}$ — the six entries with mixed indices $g_{112},g_{121},g_{122},g_{211},g_{212},g_{221}$ must all be zero (compare Problem 1 b): $[\![A,B,C]\!]=\mathbf{G}\times_1A\times_2B\times_3C$ with that superdiagonal core). To see the inclusion is strict, take $\mathbf{W}$ with $S_1=W_{::1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\ 1&0\end{pmatrix}$, $S_2=W_{::2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\ 0&0\end{pmatrix}$. (1 P. for the superdiagonal count.)

(i) A rank-$1$ tensor has every frontal slice of the form $c_k\,\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}^{\top}$, of matrix rank $\le1$; $S_1$ has rank $2$ (its determinant is $-1$), so $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{W})\ge2$.

(ii) Suppose $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{W})=2$: $\mathbf{W}=\sum_{r=1}^{2}\mathbf{a}_r\circledast\mathbf{b}_r\circledast\mathbf{c}_r$, $M_r=\mathbf{a}_r\mathbf{b}_r^{\top}$. The vectors $\mathbf{c}_1,\mathbf{c}_2$ must be independent (otherwise both slices are multiples of one matrix and the slice space is $1$-dimensional, while $S_1,S_2$ are independent), so the slice space of $\mathbf{W}$ equals $\{\alpha M_1+\beta M_2:\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}\}$ — the two descriptions differ at most by an invertible change of coordinates on the pencil. For column $j$, $\alpha M_1+\beta M_2$ has column $\alpha b_{1j}\mathbf{a}_1+\beta b_{2j}\mathbf{a}_2$, so taking the $2\times2$ determinant (a bilinear alternating form in the columns) gives

$$\det(\alpha M_1+\beta M_2)=\kappa\,\alpha\beta,\qquad \kappa=[\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2]\,[\mathbf{b}_1,\mathbf{b}_2].$$

If $\kappa=0$ the determinant vanishes identically and *every* matrix in the slice space is singular — impossible, since $S_1$ has determinant $-1$. So $\kappa\neq0$ and the singular members of the pencil are exactly the two distinct lines $\alpha=0$ and $\beta=0$. The number of singular lines is invariant under the coordinate change to the slice basis. (1 P.)

But the actual slice pencil of $\mathbf{W}$ is $\alpha S_1+\beta S_2=\begin{pmatrix}\beta&\alpha\\ \alpha&0\end{pmatrix}$ with determinant $-\alpha^2$, which vanishes only on the single line $\alpha=0$ (a double root) — one singular direction, not two. Contradiction: $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{W})\neq2$. (1 P.)

(iii) Explicitly $\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{e}_2\circledast\mathbf{e}_1\circledast\mathbf{e}_1+\mathbf{e}_1\circledast\mathbf{e}_2\circledast\mathbf{e}_1+\mathbf{e}_1\circledast\mathbf{e}_1\circledast\mathbf{e}_2$ (the three $1$-entries sit at $(2,1,1)$, $(1,2,1)$, $(1,1,2)$), so $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{W})=3$. On the other hand $\mathbf{W}$ has multilinear rank at most $(2,2,2)$ — every unfolding of a $2\times2\times2$ tensor has rank $\le2$ — so $\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{W}\times_1I_2\times_2I_2\times_3I_2$ *is* a Tucker tensor with core $(2,2,2)$; padding it into the $50\times40\times30$ corner (zero rows and columns in the factors) it lies in the $248$-number Tucker format but in no rank-$2$ CP format. The full $8$-entry core is therefore strictly more expressive than the two-entry superdiagonal core of rank-$2$ CP. (1 P.)

d) Raw: $200^3=8\,000\,000$ entries, $64\,000\,000$ bytes `float64`; the budget is $80\,000$ numbers. CP at rank $r$ stores $600r$: the largest fitting rank is $r=\lfloor 80\,000/600\rfloor=133$ ($133\cdot600=79\,800\le80\,000$, and the neighbour $134\cdot600=80\,400$ does not fit). Cubical Tucker at rank $r$ stores $r^3+600r$: $r=38$ gives $38^3+600\cdot38=54\,872+22\,800=77\,672\le80\,000$, while $r=39$ gives $59\,319+23\,400=82\,719>80\,000$, so $r=38$. Trade-off: the CP budget buys $133$ multiplicative components, but each is rank-$1$ in every mode; the Tucker budget buys $38$ component vectors per mode *plus* a free $38^3$ mixing core, so the comparison is between many simple components and fewer vectors with full cross-component interaction. (1 P.)
